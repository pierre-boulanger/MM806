Why quaternions, and not Euler angles?

Three Euler angles can reach an orientation where two of the rotation axes line up and a degree of freedom is lost — gimbal lock. A unit quaternion never locks: every orientation is one point on the unit sphere in four dimensions, and the shortest path between two of them is a clean arc. That arc is exactly what SLERP traces, which is why head-tracking and animation blend orientations as quaternions rather than as angle triples.
