Virtual Reality and Presence Quiz

MM806 Fall 2024 - Lecture 1 Review

1
According to Burdea's 1993 definition, a virtual reality system is:
Correct Answer: B
Burdea's definition emphasizes VR as a human-machine interface that creates real-time sensory experiences through multiple sensory channels (vision, audition, touch, smell, taste), not just visual graphics.
2
The "Three I's" of Virtual Reality are:
Correct Answer: C
The Three I's are fundamental characteristics that effective VR systems must possess: Immersion (technical capability), Interaction (responsiveness), and Imagination (engaging content).
3
What is the key difference between immersion and presence?
Correct Answer: B
Immersion is objective and measurable (resolution, field of view, etc.), while presence is subjective - the psychological feeling of "being there" in the virtual environment.
4
In the Reality-Virtuality Continuum, Augmented Reality (AR) is defined as:
Correct Answer: C
AR augments the real world by overlaying virtual objects onto it, while the user maintains their sense of presence in the real environment (like Pokémon GO).
5
What does "real-time" mean in the context of virtual reality?
Correct Answer: B
Real-time in VR means the system responds fast enough to user input that it appears instantaneous, preventing breaks in presence due to noticeable latency.
6
Which of the following factors is most likely to DECREASE presence in VR?
Correct Answer: C
High latency creates a noticeable delay between user actions and system response, which breaks presence and can cause motion sickness. The other options all increase presence.
7
According to research, what is true about virtual experiences and human responses?
Correct Answer: B
Research shows that virtual experiences can create real emotional and physiological responses, even with stylized graphics. This is why VR therapy works for treating phobias and PTSD.
8
Sheridan's 1992 model identifies three measurable variables that determine presence. These are:
Correct Answer: C
Sheridan's model focuses on three measurable physical variables: how much sensory information is provided, how well users can control their sensory perspective, and how much they can modify the environment.
9
Which of the following is an example of "Virtualized Reality" as discussed in the lecture?
Correct Answer: B
Virtualized Reality uses real sensor data but presents it in enhanced ways. The F-35 helmet uses real camera feeds but processes and displays them to give pilots superhuman perception abilities.
10
How can presence be measured in VR research? (Select the most complete answer)
Correct Answer: D
Presence research uses multiple complementary approaches: subjective questionnaires, psychophysical studies relating technical factors to presence ratings, and objective measures like physiological responses and behavioral observations.
